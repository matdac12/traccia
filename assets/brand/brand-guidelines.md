# Traccia Brand Identity Guidelines

**Status:** Approved working direction  
**Product:** Traccia  
**Identity concept:** Two traces. One trail.  
**Primary use today:** GitHub avatar and dashboard header

---

## 1. Brand idea

Traccia is a minimal, self-hosted issue tracker for one developer and their AI agents.

The name *Traccia* is Italian for a trace, track, trail, faint outline, plotted route, or lead to follow. The identity should express that idea without illustrating it literally.

The core visual metaphor is **two independent traces moving in the same direction**:

- the upper trace represents the human;
- the lower trace represents the agent;
- they converge toward the same route;
- they deliberately **do not touch**.

That last detail is essential. The negative space between the traces gives the mark its calmness and sense of breath. Human and agent are aligned, but remain distinct actors.

The logo should read first as **a trail or trace**, not as an illustration of “human + AI”.

---

## 2. Personality

Traccia should feel:

- technical;
- precise;
- quiet;
- sophisticated;
- restrained;
- slightly playful through the concept rather than decoration.

It should not feel corporate, loud, futuristic-for-the-sake-of-it, or like a generic AI product.

The visual benchmark is **Linear-calm** rather than “AI neon”.

---

## 3. Primary mark

The primary symbol consists of exactly **two independent strokes**.

### Upper trace

The upper trace begins horizontally, moves downward through a smooth geometric bend, and finishes horizontally.

Its primary colour is **amber**.

It represents the human trace.

### Lower trace

The lower trace begins horizontally at a lower vertical position, moves upward through a smooth geometric bend, and continues horizontally to the right.

Its primary colour is **cyan**.

It represents the agent trace.

### Relationship

The two strokes visually converge toward the same route but **never connect**.

Do not add:

- a junction;
- a bridge;
- a dot;
- a node;
- a third neutral line;
- an arrowhead;
- a shared endpoint;
- a gradient connecting the traces.

The gap is part of the logo, not accidental empty space.

---

## 4. Geometry

The geometry should be simple enough to remain recognizable at small sizes.

Use:

- consistent stroke weight;
- rounded line caps;
- smooth geometric transitions;
- primarily horizontal start and end segments;
- controlled bends rather than expressive curves;
- optical rather than purely mathematical alignment.

The upper and lower traces should feel related without becoming perfect mirror images. A small amount of asymmetry keeps the mark from feeling like a generic merge or branching icon.

The lower cyan trace may extend farther to the right than the upper amber trace. This gives the composition direction and reinforces the idea of following a trail.

---

## 5. Negative space

Negative space is a defining feature of the Traccia identity.

The two traces must remain visibly separated at every size.

At normal display sizes, preserve a gap large enough that the two colours never appear to merge due to antialiasing.

At favicon sizes, simplify geometry if necessary rather than reducing the gap until it disappears.

**Never close the gap.**

---

## 6. Primary colours

### Human / Amber

`#FF9E0B`

Purpose: the human trace.

Amber should be used intentionally. It is not a general interface accent by default.

### Agent / Cyan

`#06B6D4`

Purpose: the agent trace.

Cyan should likewise remain semantically tied to the second trace rather than becoming decorative colour throughout the product.

### Ink

`#0B0F14`

Primary dark neutral.

Use for dark surfaces, primary typography, and monochrome versions.

### Fog

`#E5E7EB`

Light neutral.

Use for subtle borders, muted surfaces, dividers, and secondary structural elements.

### White

`#FFFFFF`

Primary light background.

---

## 7. Colour principle

Every colour must do a job.

The brand should remain near-monochrome outside the two traces. Amber and cyan gain meaning because they are rare.

Avoid decorative gradients, glow effects, rainbow treatments, excessive colour coding, and generic “AI” purple-blue effects.

The primary identity is:

**amber + cyan + neutral.**

Alternative palettes may be explored later, but amber/cyan is the approved working palette.

---

## 8. Monochrome version

The symbol must also work in one flat colour.

Approved monochrome contexts include:

- black/ink on light backgrounds;
- white on dark backgrounds;
- muted neutral when the logo is intentionally secondary.

In monochrome, the two traces remain separate. Their identity comes from geometry and negative space rather than colour.

Do not join the traces simply because colour differentiation is unavailable.

---

## 9. Wordmark

The wordmark is:

`Traccia`

Use a **monospace typeface**.

The monospace treatment connects the identity to software development without turning the mark into a terminal cliché.

Desired qualities:

- clean;
- highly legible;
- restrained;
- contemporary;
- not overly mechanical;
- not retro-computing.

Suitable starting points include **JetBrains Mono**, **IBM Plex Mono**, **Geist Mono**, or a similarly calm contemporary monospace.

The approved visual direction uses moderately generous tracking. The wordmark should feel airy rather than dense.

Avoid:

- extreme letter spacing;
- stencil fonts;
- pixel fonts;
- “hacker” styling;
- terminal-green treatments;
- decorative coding symbols around the name.

---

## 10. Capitalization

Preferred:

`Traccia`

Avoid:

`TRACCIA`  
`traccia`

The capital T gives the wordmark enough structure while keeping the product quiet and approachable.

---

## 11. Primary lockup

For horizontal product contexts, place the symbol to the left of the wordmark.

Maintain generous space between symbol and wordmark.

The symbol should not visually overpower the name.

For larger identity presentations, the symbol may sit above the wordmark.

---

## 12. GitHub avatar

The GitHub avatar is currently the most important compact application.

Use:

- symbol only;
- dark near-black background;
- amber upper trace;
- cyan lower trace;
- generous internal padding.

Do not include the wordmark inside the avatar.

The mark should remain recognizable when rendered around 32 px.

---

## 13. Dashboard header

For the dashboard header:

**Dark mode**
- near-black background;
- full-colour symbol;
- light monospace wordmark.

**Light mode**
- white or very light neutral background;
- full-colour symbol;
- ink monospace wordmark.

Keep the identity small and quiet. It should identify the product rather than dominate the interface.

---

## 14. Small-size behaviour

The mark should be tested at:

- 16 px;
- 24 px;
- 32 px;
- 64 px;
- 128 px;
- 256 px.

At 32 px and above, retain the standard geometry.

At 16 px, optical adjustments are allowed:

- slightly increase stroke weight;
- slightly enlarge the gap;
- simplify the bends;
- remove non-essential geometric nuance.

Never sacrifice the separation between the two traces.

---

## 15. Clear space

Use at least **one stroke-width** of clear space around the standalone symbol.

For formal brand applications, prefer approximately **2× the stroke width**.

For the symbol + wordmark lockup, no surrounding UI element should visually enter the gap between the traces or compete with the symbol.

More space is preferable to decorative framing.

---

## 16. Backgrounds

Preferred backgrounds:

- white;
- warm/off white;
- near-black;
- restrained neutral grey.

Avoid placing the primary two-colour mark over:

- photography;
- noisy textures;
- gradients;
- saturated backgrounds;
- backgrounds that reduce amber/cyan contrast.

---

## 17. Do not

Do not:

- connect the two traces;
- turn the mark into a conventional Git branch icon;
- add arrowheads;
- add circles or nodes at the convergence;
- add a third output path;
- use gradients for their own sake;
- add shadows or glow;
- make the traces three-dimensional;
- put the mark inside a permanent badge or container;
- make the human and agent symbolism overly literal;
- add robot, spark, brain, chat-bubble, or AI-star imagery;
- force a letter T into the symbol;
- over-explain the metaphor visually.

The strength of the mark is that it remains a trace first.

---

## 18. Voice alignment

The visual identity should support Traccia's written voice:

> Calm. Precise. Short. Declarative.

Use dry humour sparingly.

Avoid hype language, exclamation marks, inflated claims, and generic AI language.

The identity should feel like a tool that is already confident in what it does.

---

## 19. Design test

Before approving any new application, ask:

1. Does this still feel quiet?
2. Are there still exactly two traces?
3. Are they clearly separate?
4. Is every colour doing a job?
5. Does it work without colour?
6. Does it remain legible at GitHub-avatar scale?
7. Does it look like Traccia rather than a generic branching or AI logo?

If the answer to any of these is no, simplify.

---

## 20. Core identity in one sentence

**Two separate traces converge on the same direction, leaving enough space between them to remain distinct.**

That is Traccia.
